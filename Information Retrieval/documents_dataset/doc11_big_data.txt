Big data refers to complex, large-volume data sets that cannot be processed using traditional data-processing application software. Big data encompasses structured, semi-structured and unstructured data, with unstructured data being the most common. The concept of big data gained momentum in the early 2000s when industry analyst Doug Laney articulated the mainstream definition of big data as the three Vs: volume, velocity and variety. Volume refers to the amount of data generated and stored. Velocity refers to the speed at which data is created and transmitted. Variety refers to the nature of the data, whether structured, semi-structured or unstructured. Big data can be analyzed for insights that lead to better decisions and strategic business moves. Big data analytics often involves advanced methods to examine large and diverse data sets to uncover hidden patterns, correlations, market trends, customer preferences and other useful information. Technologies used for big data include Hadoop, Spark, NoSQL databases and data lakes. Big data is often characterized by the five Vs: volume, velocity, variety, veracity and value. Real-world applications of big data include healthcare analytics, financial trading, marketing optimization, fraud detection and smart city planning. Big data analytics helps organizations harness their data and use it to identify new opportunities.
