Virtual reality is a simulated 3D environment that enables users to explore and interact with a virtual surrounding in a way that approximates reality as perceived through the users' senses. Virtual reality creates an immersive, computer-generated scenario that simulates experience through senses including sight and sound. The immersive environment can be similar to the real world or it can be fantastical. Virtual reality is typically achieved using a headset or head-mounted display. Headsets usually comprise a small screen in front of the eyes, motion tracking sensors, headphones and sometimes hand controllers for interaction. Virtual reality differs from traditional user interfaces by placing the user inside an experience. Users are immersed and able to interact with 3D worlds rather than viewing a screen in front of them. Virtual reality applications include gaming, education, training, healthcare, military, architecture, entertainment and art. Medical professionals use virtual reality to treat phobias, anxiety and PTSD through exposure therapy. Architects create virtual walkthroughs of buildings before construction. Virtual reality training simulators prepare pilots, soldiers and surgeons. Augmented reality differs from virtual reality by overlaying digital objects onto the real world rather than creating a fully immersive simulation.
